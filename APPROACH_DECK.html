The Problem

Residential rental pricing today is slow, inconsistent, and hard to explain to landlords or tenants.

⏱

Manual & Slow

Analysts spend 30–60 min per property pulling comps from Rightmove, Zoopla, or local knowledge. No structured process.

📊

Inconsistent

Two analysts price the same property differently. Location quality factors (schools, noise, flood risk) applied ad-hoc.

🗣

No Learning

Analyst adjustments and feedback never feed back into the pricing model. Every property starts from zero.

🌍

Not Global

Tools are UK or US-centric. International portfolios (UAE, India, Singapore) have no comparable tooling.

🔍

Not Explainable

Black-box AI pricing tools can't tell landlords why a property is priced at £1,800 vs £1,600.

📉

Revenue Leakage

Under-pricing good locations and over-pricing weaker ones leads to vacancy losses or missed income.

The Solution

RentIQ gives analysts an AI co-pilot: it generates a price, explains it, and gets smarter with every correction.

User Journey

Six steps from property details to a final agreed price — with AI assisting at every step.

1

Property Input — GPS or Address

The analyst opens RentIQ on desktop or mobile. The browser detects their GPS location and pre-fills city, postcode, and country. They can also type any address and pick from live Nominatim autocomplete suggestions — which are biased to nearby areas. Property details (bedrooms, bathrooms, size, furnishing) take 60 seconds to complete.

Browser Geolocation API Nominatim Autocomplete Auto Currency Detection
2

Location Signal Enrichment

The backend queries OpenStreetMap's Overpass API with the property's coordinates. Within 1,000m it counts schools, parks, shops, supermarkets, busy roads, rivers/flood zones, and bus stops. These are mapped to four quality scores: school quality, noise level, flood risk, and walkability. Real street names within 2,000m are fetched to generate realistic comparable addresses.

Overpass API School Count → Quality Score Road Count → Noise Score Flood Zone Detection
3

AI Comparable Generation + Pricing

Claude claude-opus-4-6 receives the property details, all 8 location signals, 6 auto-generated comparables with real street addresses, and a system prompt instructing it to price in the local currency and explain its reasoning. The response includes a recommended price, confidence score (0–100), price range (±15%), and a structured rationale. Stored learnings from previous analyst feedback are appended to the context.

Claude claude-opus-4-6 6 Real Comparables Confidence Score Structured Rationale
4

Analyst Review — Comparables Panel

The analyst sees the recommended price, confidence bar, and all 6 comparables with their address, size, rooms, and implied rent. They can remove any comparable they consider irrelevant (e.g. a luxury new-build in a mid-market area) by clicking ✕. The price instantly recalculates without the removed property. Removed comparables are tracked in session state.

Remove Comparables Instant Recalculation Session State
5

Natural Language Feedback

The analyst types free-text feedback: "This street has heavy HGV traffic — knock 5% off" or "The building has a concierge and gym — push higher". Claude parses the instruction, extracts an adjustment direction and percentage, re-prices accordingly, and shows a revised rationale. Feedback is appended to session context so the model accumulates understanding within the session.

NL Feedback Parsing Adjustment Extraction Revised Rationale
6

Learnings Stored — Closes the Loop

When the analyst confirms a final price, the feedback text and adjustment are written to the in-memory learnings log (persistent DB in production). On the next pricing session, the system prompt includes: "Past learning: analyst reduced price by 8% on busy roads in this area." Over time, the AI calibrates to local market norms without retraining.

Learnings Log In-Context Learning No Retraining Required

AI Feedback Loop

The system improves with every analyst interaction — no model retraining required.

📥 What Goes Into Claude

  • Property: bedrooms, bathrooms, m², furnishing, floor level
  • Location signals: school quality (1–5), noise (1–5), flood risk (Y/N), walkability (1–10)
  • 6 comparables: real street names, size, rooms, implied rent
  • Session feedback history (cumulative)
  • Stored learnings from previous analyst sessions
  • Currency context (auto-detected from GPS country)

📤 What Claude Returns

  • Recommended monthly rent in local currency
  • Price range (low / high estimate)
  • Confidence score 0–100
  • Bullet-point rationale (comparable-by-comparable)
  • Parsed feedback: adjustment direction + percentage
  • Revised rationale after analyst corrections

🔄 How Learning Works

  • Analyst types: "Too high — this road is very noisy"
  • Claude extracts: {direction: down, reason: road noise}
  • Adjustment applied; feedback stored in learnings log
  • Next session prompt includes the stored learning
  • AI self-calibrates to analyst's market knowledge over time
  • No fine-tuning or retraining — pure in-context learning

🚀 Production Learning (Roadmap)

  • Persist learnings in PostgreSQL per property type + area
  • Vector search: retrieve most relevant past learnings for each new property
  • Track actual achieved vs recommended price for accuracy scoring
  • Analyst override audit log for compliance reporting
  • Fine-tune embeddings on local market vocabulary

Feedback Parsing — Example

# Analyst types this in the feedback box:
"This is on a busy main road with constant lorry traffic.
 Also the building is quite dated — I'd say 7-8% below the AI price."

# Claude extracts (structured JSON in system prompt):
{
  "adjustment_direction": "down",
  "adjustment_pct": 7.5,
  "reasons": ["busy road", "dated building"],
  "confidence_delta": -8
}

# Stored learning for future sessions:
"On busy main roads in {area}, analyst typically reduces by 6-9%
 for traffic noise. Dated buildings: additional 3-5% reduction."

Intelligence Platform

  • Multi-market calibration (UK/UAE/IN)
  • Landlord-facing report generation
  • API for third-party integration
  • Claude fine-tuning on accepted prices
